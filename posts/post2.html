Substitute for Living

"I'm not goin' without you, Mac. I wouldn't leave you this way... You're coming with me."
--Chief Bromden, One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest by Ken Kesey

Lying in my new bed, first night at the Institute for Living, I hear this blood-curdling scream coming from the other end of the facility. I sit up, slumped over, head held in hands, wondering how in the world I ended up here.

The psychiatrist says I am angry with my mother. I don't think it's that. What about the war, the bad LSD trip, the loss of hope, utter despair? Nope, just mom. At least he keeps me well medicated.

One day I sit next to this older guy in our locked ward, and I ask how long have you been here. 30 years. Wow, it makes me think will I be here for thirty years, for life, lining up to take the mind-numbing meds three times a day? There is some good stuff, though, like the peanut butter and jelly sandwiches right before bedtime.

Tim, my high school buddy, has this life philosophy: It's little things that make me happy. I adopted it. Some little things are big. My friend Rick (we played on the same basketball team) calls this place the Substitute for Living. That makes me chuckle. A card from my sister has a small note scribbled at the bottom from her college roommate. It says, "I hope you feel better soon." That gives me a little smile.

One of my college friends, Howie, who was in a serious car accident a couple years earlier, comes to visit. All the patients look down the hall as the buzzer rings to let him in the ward. Howie is a big guy, with a large red beard. Because of the accident, he walks slowly, with a cane, and he shakes a lot. He is often mistaken for a drunk. The ward is silent, and no one can take their eyes off him. He sits down next to me, and says, "Do you want to go to Cape Cod and see the sunrise?" That hits me harder than electric shock treatment. Soon, I am out of the Substitute for Living.

Four years later I meet my sister's roommate, the one who wrote the little note. We are now living in Japan, happily married for 46 years and counting. Oh, and once while writing copy for cute Japanese characters, I have Hello Kitty say, "It's little things that make me happy."
