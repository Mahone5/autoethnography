Mojitos and Cigars

"Peace, that glorious moment in time when everyone stops and reloads." --Thomas Jefferson

The sixth-grade girls in the neighborhood lead the pack for the morning walk to school. I carry my lunch and baseball glove, no books, no homework. My father, self-taught and strongly against homework, says, "They have you for six-and-a-half hours a day, that's enough time for schoolwork." My mom, who makes my lunch everyday, wants me to be a doctor.

Our school prepares us well for nuclear attack. A nuke could drop on us at any moment. We have regular drills. We march down to the basement, or get under our desk. This reminds me of when I'm at work in Japan and we get under the desk for an earthquake, like that's going to help when the whole building collapses. Sonic booms are common. They are loud, and knock stuff off a shelf or sometimes even break a window. According to the Thomas Jefferson Elementary School, St. Louis is a similar size and shape to Moscow, so it's a good place to practice nuclear bomb dropping. I write a poem about the situation. "Ninety miles from our shores, lies the nearest of our nation's foes." The island of mojitos and cigars.

They teach us the three R's. After school, we play baseball, football, get into fights, loiter by the cute girl's house, and on the way home stop by Aunt Ethyl's, who gives us liberal portions of candy. I never forget the sound of her doorbell chimes. I hear the same chime in Japan, and at New Mexico State University. Then back home for dinner, and likely to chapel.

We go to chapel a lot, more than any of my school friends. Our chapel has deep, important, and dogmatic theological beliefs held by almost no one else in the world. We distinguish ourselves by calling our meeting place a chapel, definitely not a church. Our path is straight and narrow. Almost all people outside our little flock are bound for eternal damnation, where there is weeping, wailing and gnashing of teeth, forever and ever. Never ending torture. Not many attend our chapel in order to hear this Good News. In our little chapel, nuclear warfare isn't as bad as mojitos and cigars.

Thomas Jefferson Elementary School
