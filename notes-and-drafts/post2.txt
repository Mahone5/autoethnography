My first night at the Institute of Living, lying in my new bed, I hear this blood-curdling scream coming from somewhere at the other end of the facility. I sit up, slumped over, head held in hands, wondering how in the world I ended up here.

The psychiatrist says I am angry with my mother. I don't think it's that. What about the war, the bad LSD trip, the loss of hope, utter despair? Nope, just mom. He makes me angry because he keeps bringing that up; however, he does keep me well medicated. 

One day I sit next to this older guy in our locked ward, and I ask how long have you been here. 30 years. Wow, it makes me think will I be here for 30 years, for life, lining up to take the mind-numbing meds three times a day? There is some good stuff, though, like the peanut butter and jelly sandwiches right before bedtime.

Tim my high school buddy has this life philosphy: It's little things that make me happy. I have adopted it. Some little things are big. My friend Rick (we played on the same basketball team) calls this place the Substitute for Living. That gives me a chuckle. A card from my sister has a small note scribbled at the bottom from her college roommate. It says, "I hope you feel better soon." That gives me a little smile.

One of my college friends, Howie, who was in a serious car accident a couple years earlier, comes to visit. All the patients look down the hall as the buzzer rings to let him in the ward. Because of the accident, Howie walks slowly, with a cane. He is often mistaken for a drunk. No one could take their eyes off of him. He sits down next to me, and says, "Do you want to go to Cape Cod and see the sunrise?" That hits me harder than electric shock treatment. Soon, I am out of the Substitute for Living.

Four years later I meet my sister's Japanese roommate, the one who wrote the little note. 46 years later we are still happily married, living in Japan. There's something about those little things.
